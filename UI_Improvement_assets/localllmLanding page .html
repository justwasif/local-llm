The problem

The barrier is the interface, not the person.

Information about school, crops, health and public services often arrives in a language people don’t use comfortably, or in a tool that only works in one. When a system can’t meet people where they are, the gap belongs to the system. LocalLLM explores a language-first alternative.

Ask naturally

Write the way you normally write — in an Indic language, or mixed with English.

Choose the reply language

You pick the language you want the answer in. There is no automatic language detection.

Start from everyday topics

Education, healthcare, agriculture and government services.

PS4

Low-resource and multilingual languages. The track asks teams to improve a model for underserved languages, dialects or code-mixed text in a public-facing setting such as education, public services, agriculture or healthcare. That is the problem LocalLLM is exploring.

How it works

Pick a language and a topic. Then just ask.

1

Choose language and topic

Select one of 12 reply-language options and one of four topics.

2

Write, or tap a sample

Type your question naturally, or start from a sample prompt.

3

Read a streamed reply

The response streams in. The prompt asks the model to answer in the language you selected, unless you ask otherwise.

How it’s built

A small model, trained and served locally.

The track requires SGLang as the core serving engine and calls for CuratorKIT, AlignTune and AuditKIT across curation, fine-tuning and evaluation.

Status

Where it honestly stands.

What it is

  • A research prototype exploring language access.
  • A 1.5B-parameter model fine-tuned locally with SFT and DPO.
  • A language-first interface: you choose the reply language and topic, then ask.

What it isn’t (yet)

  • Validated as a reliable source of advice.
  • A public demo — the development server binds to loopback only.
  • Broad language coverage — the training sample is small and uneven.
  • Automatic language detection — you choose the reply language.
Health
